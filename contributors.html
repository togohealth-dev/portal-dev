Method & honesty notes

Hours are estimated per project, not per line. A project that touches only the frontend counts 2.5h; one that touches the Rust backend or a migration counts 11h. That is the whole formula — there is no lines-of-code term.

Why line count was dropped. Earlier versions charged a flat 55 lines/hr. Measured against what people actually remember spending, that model was badly wrong in both directions: Riley wrote 531 effective lines in one project that took about 3h (177 lines/hr), while Morgan wrote 405 lines across four projects that took about 10h (40 lines/hr). Same file types, same stack, a 4× difference in rate — so lines were measuring typing, not effort. What actually predicts the number is how many separate projects you carried and whether each one reached the backend: understanding the problem, wiring it in, running it on localhost, and the PR round trip cost roughly the same whether the diff is 14 lines or 800.

Calibration and its limits. The two rates are fitted to three anecdotal totals from Greg — Greg ~50h, Morgan ~10h, Riley ~3h — and reproduce them to within 0.6h in aggregate (50.5 / 10.0 / 2.5). That is three data points against two parameters, so the fit is close to saturated: treat the shape (per-project cost, backend multiplier ~4×) as the finding, and the exact 2.5 and 11 as provisional. Every completed project that gets its real hours recorded tightens them.

Attribution is by PR author. Commit authorship was unusable — 77 of 110 commits are recorded as invalid-email-address, mapping to no GitHub account. /pull-pr-code-only rewrites authorship to whoever ran the import, so a synced PR credits the importer; #20 (MFA) was corrected to Scott on that basis and is the one known case. The other synced PRs are treated as their author’s own upstream work.

Scott is excluded from this summary. He reviews and gatekeeps rather than being counted as a build contributor here, so the projects whose PRs he opened do not appear.

Nineteen completed projects are missing entirely — the early batch (#59–#72 and others) has no linked PR, so there is nothing to attribute. This page covers the 15 shipped projects that have one, plus 2 still in the pipeline.

Stage lines count “reached this stage or beyond”, not “carries this label”. Only 3 of the 15 shipped projects ever got a status:beta-version label and two (#191, #141) never got an alpha one — the labels record what someone clicked, not what the code did. Matching labels exactly left the β line dangling below the γ line (Greg at 5h beta against 50.5h shipped) and gave Riley no α line at all, which made the pipeline gap unreadable and put stray green and blue segments down in the 2.5h band where they looked like someone else’s work. A row now counts toward every stage it reached or passed, dated by its own label when there is one and otherwise by the earliest later stage on record. The cost of that fix: where a β label is missing, the dashed line steps on the production date, so it reads as “no recorded time in beta” rather than a measured beta date.

Two contributors are α-only so far. Jordan (#486, scheduling capacity targets, α 17 Aug) and Eric (#344 Part 1, Allergies + GFR above the order box, α 19 Aug) have each reached PR alpha-ready without shipping to γ, so they appear on the dotted line and nowhere else. That is the chart working as intended rather than a gap in the data — but it does mean their totals are a floor: the β and γ lines will pick the same hours up again as Scott promotes the work, and the horizontal run of their dotted line is pipeline latency, not idle time.

Eric has three more PRs open that are not counted yet — #510 and #514 (UHIN contract and pull-side design, docs) and #512 (the HL7 parser, Rust backend). Their cards #173 and #307 are still status:in-progress, so they have no α date to plot. #307 is a backend project, so it will add 11h the moment it reaches alpha — more than four times what Part 1 of #344 scored. His cargo fmt sweep + CI gate (#513) has no tracker card at all and so cannot be attributed under a per-project model.

Neither new contributor is calibrated. The 2.5h / 11h rates were fitted to Greg's recollection of his own, Morgan's and Riley's time. Eric's and Jordan's hours are the model's prediction with nothing anecdotal behind them — both happen to be frontend-only projects, so both land on 2.5h, and neither has been checked against what they actually spent. The model also has no rate for documentation: Eric's #510 is 815 lines of partner-facing API spec, which is neither frontend nor backend, and would score 2.5h by default if its card reached alpha today.
